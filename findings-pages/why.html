Why might one stack be faster?

We can show where the two stacks differ. We cannot yet show why.

The recordings show the tilt each drone was told to reach and the tilt it reached. They do not show what happened inside the flight software.

What does "8 kHz versus 1.6 kHz" really mean?

It does not mean Betaflight senses or controls five times faster. It is how often the simulator exchanges messages with each stack. Select a row to see what it means.

What runs how often in these simulations
WhatBetaflightCogniPilot
Messages with the simulator8,000 per second1,600 per second
New motion sensor data1,600 per second1,600 per second
Control and motor updates500 per secondNot recorded
Stick commands250 per second250 per second
Position feedback100 per second100 per second

The 500 per second figure comes from internal Betaflight snapshots of two flights. Betaflight ran at that rate with the PWM motor protocol, which was our test setting. A different protocol might allow faster updates, but we have not confirmed that.

The message difference could change timing by up to about 0.5 milliseconds. The drone takes about 167 milliseconds to respond to a tilt command at K = 6/s. So the message difference is about 0.3 percent of that time. It is a limit we must disclose, not a likely explanation.

    Nobody has retested K = 6/s on this test bed. Changing it would need a rebuild and a full rerun for both stacks, so we keep it and say so.

    What would explain the result?

    We can compare three things without new firmware:

    1. Each stack against an ideal attitude response.
    2. Tilt lead on against tilt lead off for each stack.
    3. Commanded tilt against actual tilt, from the 40 s pair.

    Separating the estimator, filters and control loop would need new measurements inside the firmware. That would change the firmware and force a rerun of every test, so it is not part of this week's work.
